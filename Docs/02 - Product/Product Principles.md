These principles protect the identity of Human Drift. Every feature and  
journey should be consistent with them.

## 1. Continuity of meaning, not productivity

Human Drift is not a productivity optimizer.

Its purpose is to preserve the relationship between what a person  
intended and what actually happened across time and changing  
circumstances.

## 2. Intention survives time

An intention does not disappear merely because activity stops.

Inactivity is evidence, not proof of abandonment.

If the person deliberately changes or closes an intention, that change  
is recorded explicitly.

## 3. Original intention is preserved

The system never silently rewrites what was originally intended.

A later revision is a new fact.

The product must always be able to distinguish: - original intention; -  
revised intention; - actual execution.

## 4. Reality is evidence

Human Drift records what happened before deciding what it means.

A logged event is not automatically an interpretation.

## 5. Observe before inferring

The system should collect sufficient evidence and context before  
classifying drift or other behavioral patterns.

One missed action is not automatically meaningful drift.

## 6. Context before interruption

The system should understand the surrounding situation before  
interrupting, notifying, or acting.

A technically correct interruption at the wrong moment can still be a  
bad product behavior.

## 7. Explainable autonomy

Every autonomous action should have an understandable reason.

The user should be able to answer: - What did the system do? - What  
caused it? - What permission allowed it? - What evidence was available?

## 8. Historical integrity

History is part of the product.

Corrections preserve the original value. Changes are recorded.  
Contradictions are surfaced instead of silently erased.

## 9. Flexible structure

Human work does not always fit a fixed hierarchy.

Human Drift therefore uses a universal Node model with flexible nesting.

Do not force every situation into: **Project → Task → Subtask**

A hierarchy should reflect observed meaning and relationships.

## 10. Discovery is part of work

Unexpected ideas and newly surfaced work are not noise by default.

A discovery can become a first-class Node and preserve its lineage to  
the SessionEntry where it emerged.

The system should distinguish: - intended work; - emergent work; - later  
assignment or organization of that work.

## 11. Evidence before optimization

Do not optimize a behavior before understanding it.

Real usage should challenge assumptions about: - hierarchy; - context; -  
conditions; - logging friction; - drift; - autonomy.

## 12. Human authority

Human Drift may assist, infer, and eventually act autonomously, but it  
must not quietly replace the person's authority over their intentions.

## What these principles protect against

- becoming a guilt machine;
    
- becoming a habit tracker with a different name;
    
- treating every life change as a failure;
    
- rewriting history to make behavior look cleaner;
    
- forcing all work into rigid task structures;
    
- autonomous behavior the user cannot explain;
    
- optimizing before sufficient evidence exists.
